## Acceptance contract

1. Nonresponse surfaces without anybody compiling a list, read from the
   `nonresponse_queue` view that already exists on `main`.
2. The queue lives **under Administration** in the operator shell and reuses the
   application's own table, filters and chrome rather than a second visual
   language.
3. It lists every unanswered and every undeliverable person across approved
   events. Undeliverable people are labelled as a delivery problem and repaired
   by W6 — one list, two streams.
4. Each person carries the chase position W4 renders.
5. Escalation fires at the response deadline plus N hours, N configurable per
   event type, N = 0 permitted. W7 owns where N is set.
6. The escalation resolves to an **office**. Whoever holds the President's seat
   when it fires receives it; changing the seat changes the recipient with no
   configuration change. A vacant seat holds it visibly rather than dropping it
   or sending to a stale holder.
7. The escalation body carries **no name, contact detail or reason**. It states
   how many people, which event, and the deadline, then links to the queue.
8. **One alert per person per event, ever.** Reruns, retries, crashes and
   backlogs never produce a second one.
9. A flag clears only by real resolution — an answer, a recorded answer, or a
   repaired delivery — never by time passing.
10. A cleared flag remains readable in history, because the record that the club
    escalated is evidence.
11. Answering removes the row and clears the flag in the same transaction.
12. The queue is operator-tier and unreachable without a seat.
13. The escalation itself, if it fails to send, is a delivery failure like any
    other and appears in W6 rather than being discarded.
