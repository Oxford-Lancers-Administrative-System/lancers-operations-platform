## Acceptance contract

1. The event page shows the complete event details first and the named invited
   audience second. W1 does not replace or isolate either Mission 2 surface.
2. The messaging plan appears last as an expandable disclosure. Its summary is
   useful while closed, and expanding it shows every resolved send time.
3. The default nonresponse sequence is WhatsApp message 1, WhatsApp message 2,
   email, then follow-up escalation to the President. Each later user-facing
   message excludes people who have already answered.
4. No quiet-hours rule delays, rolls or drops any step.
5. Before approval, a missing or unusable WhatsApp route appears as a concise
   count: “1 user has an error.” “See 1 user with error” reveals the affected
   person. W1 offers no manual-send workaround; W6 owns correction and recovery.
6. Approval freezes the audience and the displayed plan and creates the planned
   jobs atomically. The confirmation reports jobs created and immediate errors
   without claiming that scheduled messages have already been delivered.
7. Every started job reaches a recorded terminal outcome or a visible recovery
   state. Failed sends retry automatically; remaining errors are available from
   the existing Delivery surface.
8. The displayed plan and scheduler agree for each event type and for fixed-clock
   normal, short-runway and already-past-deadline cases. Compression specifics
   remain governed and accepted in `W7`.
9. The approval plan, concise error disclosure and approved-plan state render at
   a true 375px width without horizontal scrolling. The event details and invited
   audience remain ahead of the plan at that breakpoint.
10. An operator without `event_approval` cannot see or invoke the approval action.
    Reading the projected plan creates no invitations, jobs or delivery attempts.
